01

Revenue leakage

6 questions

What is revenue leakage in a professional services firm?

Revenue leakage is money a firm earned, or could have earned, that never reached an invoice or a bank account. It covers unbilled time, enquiries nobody followed up, renewals that lapsed, discounts nobody approved and agreed fees that were never raised. It is different from bad debt, which is an invoice that was raised and then not paid.

SourcesAccelo: what is revenue leakage

How much revenue do professional services firms lose to leakage?

Published research puts the loss at roughly 4% to 5% of revenue. A 4.3% figure is quoted by Accelo and Sage, Certinia cites 4% to 5%, and a Kantata and IDC study found firms that fail to adopt professional services automation software lose 5% to 10%. On £4m of annual revenue, 4.3% is £172,000 a year. These are industry averages. A Leak Scan measures your own firm from your own data.

SourcesAcceloSageCertiniaKantata and IDC via Business Wire

Where does revenue leakage usually start?

At the handoffs between people and systems. An enquiry arrives and nobody owns the follow-up. A client conversation reveals extra work that never reaches a quote. A renewal date passes without a call. Time is recorded but the invoice goes out weeks later. Each gap is small. Added together they are the 4% to 5% that research keeps finding.

How do I know if my firm is leaking revenue?

Compare three pairs of numbers for the last twelve months: hours recorded against hours billed, enquiries received against instructions opened, and renewals due against renewals kept. A gap in any pair is leakage. If your system cannot produce those pairs in under an hour, that is a finding in itself. A free Leak Scan builds all three from an export of your own data.

Is revenue leakage the same as bad debt?

No. Bad debt is an invoice that was raised and never paid. Leakage happens before the invoice or around it: work never billed, billed below the agreed rate, or an opportunity nobody pursued. Both reduce cash, but they need different fixes. Bad debt needs credit control and collection. Leakage needs visibility and a named owner for each handoff.

What is a realisation rate and why does it matter?

Realisation rate compares what a firm bills, or collects, with the value of the time recorded at standard rates. A rate below 100% means time is being discounted, written off or left unbilled. Track billing realisation and collection realisation separately, because a firm can bill well and still collect badly, or the other way round.

02

Lock-up and WIP

6 questions

What is lock-up in a law firm or accountancy practice?

Lock-up is the money tied up in work already done: unbilled work in progress plus billed invoices not yet paid, expressed as days of fee income. The Law Society and Hazlewoods 2026 Financial Benchmarking Survey reports a median lock-up of 134 days, down from 146 the year before. Including disbursements the median is 144 days, down from 156.

SourcesLaw Society: Financial Benchmarking Survey 2026

What lock-up target should a firm aim for?

SW Group reports that just under half of firms target 75 days or less. Lubbock Fine, reported by City AM, puts lock-up at the top 100 firms at 124 days. Against a 2026 median of 134 days for firms of all sizes, a 75-day target is ambitious, so set the target by billing model: fixed-fee work and hourly work lock up differently.

SourcesSW Group: the key to lock-upCity AM: Lubbock Fine lock-up

What is WIP and how is it valued?

Work in progress is time and cost spent on client work that has not yet been invoiced. Under FRS 102 section 23, revenue from services is recognised by reference to the stage of completion, which is why WIP needs a defensible valuation at every period end. An unreviewed WIP balance is both a cash-flow risk and an accounting risk.

SourcesVJM Global: accounting for WIP in UK professional services

Why is aged WIP a risk?

The older unbilled time gets, the less of it a firm bills in full. BigHand research found 50% of firms now name aged WIP as their primary cash-flow pressure, up from 32% in the previous survey. Age WIP in buckets of 0 to 30, 31 to 60, 61 to 90 and over 90 days, and give every matter over 60 days a named owner.

SourcesLegal Futures: BigHand research

What causes WIP write-offs?

The usual causes are billing late, scope that grew without a written change, time entries a client will not accept because the narrative is thin, fixed-fee overruns and disputes raised only when the invoice lands. Most are visible weeks earlier in the data, which is why a weekly review of ageing WIP prevents more write-offs than any month-end clean-up.

SourcesActionstep: write-offsEnterprise DNA: reduce WIP write-offs

How can a firm reduce lock-up days?

Pull three levers in order. Bill sooner, by shortening the age of WIP before it is invoiced. Collect faster, by chasing debtors on a fixed rhythm. Then stop the leak upstream, so less unbilled work builds up at all. Measure all three weekly, not monthly, because lock-up is a lagging number and the weekly figures show the change first.

Data and AI safety

7 questions

Is our client data safe if IntelMadeSimple analyses it?

IntelMadeSimple tokenises data before any AI processing. Names, emails and identifiers are replaced with tokens, and the mapping between token and person never leaves your control. A data processing agreement is signed with every engagement, and a data protection impact assessment is available on request.

Is tokenised data still personal data under UK GDPR?

Treat it as personal data. The ICO's final anonymisation guidance, published on 28 March 2025, confirms that pseudonymised data remains personal data when someone can re-identify it. Tokenisation reduces risk but does not remove your UK GDPR duties, which is why a processing agreement and a lawful basis still apply.

SourcesBird & Bird: key takeaways from the ICO anonymisation guidance

Do we need a DPIA before using AI on client data?

Often yes, or at least a screening assessment. UK GDPR requires a DPIA where processing is likely to create a high risk to individuals, and new technology and large-scale processing both count as triggers. IntelMadeSimple supplies a DPIA on request. Your data protection officer or compliance lead makes the final call.

What did the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 change?

The Act received Royal Assent on 19 June 2025. It amends UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 rather than replacing them, and its provisions commence in phases. For a firm using AI on client data, the practical effect is that the core duties stay in place: lawful basis, transparency, security and accountability.

SourcesClyde & Co: the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025

Does IntelMadeSimple send our data to third-party AI models?

Only with your written consent. Without it, no third-party model touches your data. The full position is in the IntelMadeSimple AI policy.

SourcesIntelMadeSimple AI policy

Which security and AI standards does IntelMadeSimple follow?

IntelMadeSimple aligns its practice with ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO/IEC 42001, the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, Cyber Essentials, and UK GDPR with the Data Protection Act 2018. This is alignment, not certification, and we say so on every page where it matters.

SourcesIntelMadeSimple AI policy

Will AI make decisions about our clients or our fees?

No. Any decision that touches money, people or compliance gets human review. The tools find, rank and draft. A named person at your firm approves before anything is sent, billed or changed.
